# Flores Garden OS — product map

## What we are carrying over from the studio OS

### 1. Owner home / command centre
The studio's strongest pattern is a single owner page that surfaces money, upcoming work and things requiring action. Flores keeps that pattern but changes the metrics to:
- paid this month
- upcoming jobs
- new enquiries
- customers
- recurring work
- next visits

### 2. CRM
The studio's customer/contact database becomes a garden-centric CRM:
- customer
- postcode
- property/garden notes
- contact details
- service history
- recurring status
- future potential

### 3. Jobs + quotes
Studio bookings become garden jobs. The important distinction is that a job can be:
- enquiry
- quote
- scheduled
- in progress
- completed
- cancelled

The eventual quote builder should understand service-specific pricing rather than forcing every job into a generic hourly rate.

### 4. Calendar / operations
The calendar remains a core owner tool, but the gardening version should eventually add:
- travel time
- postcode clustering
- weather considerations
- recurring route days
- waste/disposal requirements
- equipment requirements

### 5. Analytics
The studio analytics pattern is retained, but the KPIs are gardening-specific:
- lead → quote
- quote → booked
- average job value
- revenue per working hour
- recurring customer rate
- service revenue
- postcode revenue
- lead source
- route density
- seasonal demand

### 6. Marketing
The same CRM/marketing architecture can eventually support:
- Google Business reviews
- seasonal campaigns
- autumn clearance campaigns
- winter fruit-tree pruning
- spring planting
- wildlife-garden campaigns
- lapsed-customer reactivation

### 7. Accounting / payments
The studio's money layer maps cleanly to gardening, but should ultimately add:
- invoices
- deposits
- Stripe payments
- expenses
- green-waste costs
- materials/plant costs
- fuel/travel
- gross margin by job

## What we are intentionally not carrying over

- artist / engineer terminology
- recording sessions
- mixes / masters
- dry-hire workflows
- studio-hour credits
- music-specific delivery systems
- artist development

The goal is to keep the **operating-system quality** without accidentally making the gardening site feel like a reskinned studio app.

## Visual direction

The supplied Flores mark becomes the visual anchor. The public site uses a natural cream/green palette and editorial typography. The owner OS uses the same brand but becomes denser and more operational.
